Every small beauty business knows the conversation. A customer sees a product, sends a message, asks for the price, checks which shade is available, and comes back hours later to ask about delivery.

There is nothing wrong with a conversation. Often, the personal touch is the reason people buy from an independent seller in the first place. But repeating the same information all day leaves less time for everything else.

## Give the basics a home

Start with the questions that appear in your inbox most often. Price, size, shade, ingredients, availability, and how to use the product should be easy to find without asking.

A useful product page does not need to sound like an advertisement. Think of it as the answer you would give a customer in your shop: clear, friendly, and specific.

- Photograph the actual product in good light.
- Show the size and any available variants.
- Explain who the product is for without making unsupported claims.
- Keep stock availability up to date.
- Say what is included, and what is not.

## Make your store link do some of the work

Your social accounts do not need to disappear. They can become the introduction rather than the entire buying journey.

Put a consistent store link in your profile and include it when someone asks about your collection. Use direct product links when you are discussing a particular item. A customer should not have to search a whole catalogue to find the thing you just showed them.

> Keep the personal conversation. Give the repeated information somewhere to live.

## Set expectations before the order

A first-time customer is looking for reassurance. Explain your payment arrangements, delivery area, dispatch schedule, and policy on returns before asking them to commit.

If an item is made to order or temporarily unavailable, say so. A realistic expectation is more valuable than a quick promise you may struggle to keep.

## A manageable next step

Pick five products you sell most often. Write complete descriptions for them, organise the variants, and take a consistent set of photographs.

You do not have to rebuild your entire business in a weekend. A small, well-presented collection is a good place to begin.
