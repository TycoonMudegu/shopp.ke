Fresh produce has its own clock. What arrived this morning might need to find a customer today. Meanwhile, orders arrive through calls, messages, regular walk-ins, and neighbours passing by.

The challenge is not just attracting more customers. It is making the demand you already have a little easier to plan for.

## Make availability a daily habit

An online catalogue should reflect what you can actually supply. Start the day by checking the products, quantities, and prices you have listed.

For groceries, the details that seem obvious at the counter are especially useful online. Is the price per kilo, per bunch, per tray, or per item? Can the weight vary? Are substitutions possible?

A little clarity saves an uncomfortable conversation later.

## Give orders a realistic cut-off

An order cut-off helps customers understand what to expect and gives you time to plan picking and packing.

Choose a schedule your business can support. Avoid promising a same-day window unless you can consistently fulfil it, including time for the actual delivery route.

- State the order cut-off where customers can see it.
- Confirm any product substitutions before charging for them.
- Separate ambient, chilled, and delicate items appropriately.
- Provide storage information where it is useful.

## Start with a useful bundle

A simple vegetable box or weekly essentials collection can make a catalogue easier to browse. The value is not necessarily a discount. Sometimes it is the convenience of knowing what arrives.

Keep the contents clear. If a seasonal item changes, communicate the replacement instead of leaving the customer to discover it at the door.

> A predictable order is easier to prepare than a collection of unfinished conversations.

## Keep the feedback loop close

Ask customers whether the quantities, packaging, and delivery timing worked for them. Look for repeated requests before expanding your range.

The aim is a more dependable rhythm for the business: clearer orders, better preparation, and customers who know how to buy from you again.
